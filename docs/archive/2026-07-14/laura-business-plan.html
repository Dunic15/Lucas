Laura; in three parts.

An AI process avatar you call into Zoom, Meet and Teams. It listens, answers straight from your own company docs, and does the follow-up. Not a notetaker; an AI employee.

What it is → How it works → What it costs
1 · The problem

The one person who knows the process is never in the room. Approvals get skipped, decisions stall, the critical step is missed; then someone loses the next day chasing the answer down.

2 · The solution

You call Laura into the meeting. She knows the process cold, answers straight from your docs and cites them on the spot, flags the step you're about to skip, and does the follow-up herself.

3 · Why it matters

Knowledge workers already lose ~1 day every week just searching for information, and 56% have to interrupt a colleague or book a meeting to get an answer. Laura is the colleague who's always in the room. - McKinsey · Atlassian

Why not just a notetaker?

A notetaker records the meeting. Laura is in the meeting.

Notetakers are a rear-view mirror; they transcribe what was said. They can't answer a question, they don't know your process, and they can't act. Different category, different willingness to pay.

  • Flag the missed step in a summary nobody reads

present · cited · acts

1

What it is

You invite Laura to a meeting like a colleague. During the call it answers questions straight from your process docs, with citations. After the call it delivers an artifact: summary, decisions, actions, a readiness score, and a draft follow-up email.

2

How it works

The whole flow, from a human inviting Laura to the avatar speaking back in the meeting; and the artifact afterwards. The brain is ours; the meeting bot, face and voice are swappable parts around it.

1
Human

You invite Laura

Add the avatar to a calendar event, or drop it into a live call. It joins like any other participant, with a name, a face and a voice.

2
Meeting bot · Recall

A bot joins the call; its ears & camera

Recall.ai puts a bot in Zoom/Meet/Teams. It streams the live transcript to us, and renders our avatar page as the bot's camera.

3
The brain · our backend

Transcript → our backend

Every line of speech reaches our FastAPI backend in real time. This is the part that thinks; everything else is plumbing.

4
The brain

It reads your docs & tracks the meeting

It pulls the relevant passages from your process docs and the history of your past calls, the Granola / Otter notes and prior transcripts included, so it always has the context to keep the conversation on point and answer correctly. Laura doesn't replace your notetaker; she works alongside it, turning its record into live context. In parallel she silently tracks the meeting against a checklist, required steps, decisions, owners, with zero added latency.

5
The brain

It decides when to speak, then answers with sources

It stays silent unless addressed. When it speaks, the answer is drawn straight from your docs and cited back to them; fast (first word in well under a second), never a blocking pause.

6
Face + voice

The avatar speaks it back

The answer is spoken by the avatar's face and voice, back in the meeting; the open-source in-browser face by default, a photoreal face as a premium upgrade.

7
After the call

The artifact & dashboard

Summary, decisions, actions with owners, missing steps, a readiness score, and a draft follow-up email. It all lands in the dashboard; an admin panel showing every meeting Laura joined, the answers she gave and their sources, the actions she took, and the follow-ups still open across the team.

Why it's a moat

The brain lives in our backend; the avatar is only a commandable mouth. Adding an avatar = adding a folder; no new code. Defensibility is in knowledge grounding + process templates + verticalization, not the meeting plumbing (which is commoditizing).

Read the estimate this way

At ~$0.014/min to run and $59–149+/seat to sell, a seat covers its meeting cost dozens of times over; the real cost is acquisition, not delivery. Removing the paid third-party avatar face is what fixed the model: under that old ~$0.12/min face, Pro would have been negative (20 hrs alone > $59). The photoreal face now adds under $0.25/30-min, so it's a cheap premium, not a margin killer. Willingness-to-pay anchor: the person who owns a process (RevOps/HR/IT) costs $80–130k/year. Laura is priced as a fraction of one hire. Numbers are ballpark, grounded in verified 2026 vendor rates; full model in docs/finance/cost-model.md.
