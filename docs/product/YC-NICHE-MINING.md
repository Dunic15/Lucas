# YC Niche Mining — where a "process-avatar in the meeting + executes the follow-up" wins

> **Purpose:** narrow Laura to ONE validated vertical, grounded in what real YC
> companies (~2022–2026 batches) actually build and who they sell to.
> **Method (Jacopo's):** scan recent YC batches → find the SERVICE industries the
> companies cater to → pick a big + already-validated + approachable one where
> humans are expensive and meetings are dense with action items.
> **Status:** research memo, market-intel. Numbers marked *(directional)* are not
> exact — YC directory counts move and some are single-source. Do not commit.
> **Owner-relay date:** 2026-07-14.

---

## Executive summary (read this)

1. **The winning vertical is Recruiting / Staffing.** It scores highest on every
   criterion the advisors set: expensive humans, meeting-and-action density, market
   size, and YC validation — with **one caveat** on regulation (below).
2. **YC has poured startups into it and buyers pay a lot:** Alex (AI recruiter)
   raised a **$17M Series A** to automate screening calls; Contrario reports
   **~$6M ARR** *(directional, company-stated)*; Perfectly, Spott, Saffron,
   HeyMilo, ConverzAI, Ribbon all attack the same wallet. YC lists **~58 recruiting
   startups** *(directional)*.
3. **The regulation caveat, stated honestly:** recruitment AI is a *named high-risk
   category* under the EU AI Act. That conflicts with "avoid regulated" — BUT it is
   a **transparency/documentation** obligation, not a **licensing wall** (unlike
   insurance/wealth/healthcare, where you literally cannot operate without a
   license or HIPAA). It doesn't apply to a US-first launch, and it's avoidable by
   scoping Laura to *internal hiring-team ops*, not candidate scoring.
4. **So the defensible entry is NOT the candidate-facing interview** (crowded —
   Alex et al. already own it) but the **recruiter's internal meetings** — the
   client/hiring-manager **intake call** and the **interview debrief** — where the
   expensive humans meet and the follow-up actions pile up (JD, scheduling, ATS
   writes, submittal/recap emails, scorecards).
5. **Runners-up:** consultative B2B **Sales** (discovery/demo) and **Consulting /
   professional-services delivery** — both lower-regulation, both weaker on
   "one big validated vertical" than recruiting.
6. **Avoided on purpose:** Insurance brokerage, Wealth/financial advisory,
   Healthcare admin — all hot at YC, all gated by licensing/HIPAA. Jacopo was right.
7. **Honest dependency:** this pick assumes Laura ships the **action-execution
   layer**, which today is *deferred* (WEDGE.md). The advisors' framing IS a build,
   not a current capability.

## Top-3 deep dives — the actions the avatar would execute, and the knowledge it needs

### 1) Recruiting / Staffing — the recruiter's INTERNAL meetings (intake + debrief)

**Why it's #1 (grounded in real YC companies).** This is the single most-validated
vertical-AI lane at YC right now, which is exactly what "already-validated" means in
Jacopo's method:
- **Alex** (YC) — AI recruiter that runs *live video screening interviews*, "thousands
  of interviews daily for Fortune 100," **$17M Series A** led by Peak XV. Competitors
  cited alongside it: **HeyMilo, ConverzAI, Ribbon.**
- **Contrario** (YC) — recruiters + vertical AI agents; **~$6M annualized revenue,
  150+ placements** *(directional, company-stated)*.
- **Perfectly** (YC) — "first AI-native Recruiting OS" (sourcing → screening → qual).
- **Spott** (YC) — "AI-native ATS/CRM for recruiting & staffing firms" — proof the
  *staffing-firm buyer* is a real, paid wallet.
- YC directory lists **~58 recruiting startups** *(directional)*.

**The expensive human being replaced/augmented.** Agency/exec-search recruiters bill
**15–25% of first-year salary** ($15–40k+/placement) or **$50k+ retainers**; their
meeting time literally *is* the billable product. In-house TA is a large cost center.

**The wedge that dodges the crowd.** Alex/HeyMilo already own *candidate-facing*
screening — do NOT enter there. Enter the meetings they don't touch: the **client/
hiring-manager intake** and the **interview debrief/calibration**, where the panel is
expensive, the discussion is candid, and the follow-ups multiply.

**Actions the avatar executes (in/after the meeting):**
1. **Draft & post the job spec/JD** from the intake call's captured requirements →
   push to ATS / careers page / LinkedIn (draft-then-approve).
2. **Schedule** — send scheduling links to candidate + panel; book rooms; sequence
   rounds (calendar action).
3. **Write to the ATS/CRM** (Ashby / Greenhouse / Bullhorn / Recruit CRM): create the
   req, move stages, attach the structured scorecard.
4. **Debrief scorecard + decision** — turn the debrief into a structured
   advance/reject with owner + next step; **flag once** if the panel skipped a
   must-have competency (Laura's existing missing-step intervention, retargeted).
5. **Send the submittal / prep / rejection email** — candidate summary to the client,
   or prep/rejection to the candidate (drafted, human-approve-then-send).
6. **Chase & SLA** — nudge the interviewer who owes feedback; flag stalled reqs.

**Knowledge the avatar needs (the moat, per WEDGE.md):** the firm's hiring playbook,
leveling/competency rubric, structured-interview question bank, EEO/GDPR-safe
questioning guardrails, the specific req + intake brief, per-client account
preferences, submittal/offer templates, ATS field mapping.

**Adversarial check — what makes recruiting the WRONG niche:**
- **Most-crowded, best-funded lane at YC.** A pre-revenue avatar can be lapped; and
  the ATS incumbents (Ashby/Greenhouse/Bullhorn) are shipping their own meeting AI +
  action execution — the classic "native platform eats the horizontal feature" threat.
- **EU AI Act high-risk.** The opposite of "approachable." Only survivable by scoping
  to internal ops (no candidate scoring) and launching US-first.
- **WTP for the *internal* slice is less proven** than candidate throughput (where
  Alex actually sells). Staffing-agency buyers are price-sensitive SMBs; enterprise TA
  has long procurement.

### 2) Consultative B2B Sales — AE/SE discovery & demo calls

**Why (grounded in YC):** the in-meeting-copilot model is already YC-validated —
**Caretta** ("Sales Intelligence… **joins reps in live calls**, helping them handle
calls"), **Simple AI** ("AI voice that sells"), **Kanava AI** ("first AI sales hire
for wholesale distributors"); plus the AI-SDR wave (**AiSDR, Gojiberry, Throxy** —
~24/28/23 AI-SDR cos per batch in 2023/24/25, *directional*). Highest raw WTP after
recruiting (AEs/SEs at $150–300k OTE). Low regulation.

**Actions the avatar executes:**
1. Send the **recap/next-steps email** from the call (MEDDIC/BANT fields captured).
2. **Update the CRM** (HubSpot/Salesforce): stage, next step, close date, contacts.
3. **Draft the proposal/quote** or one-pager from discovery answers.
4. **Schedule** the next step (technical deep-dive, POC kickoff) + owners.
5. **Flag a missing qualification** live (no budget owner named, no decision date) —
   Laura's missing-step intervention as a deal-hygiene guardrail.
6. **Route** security/pricing follow-ups to the right internal owner.

**Knowledge it needs:** the company's product/pricing/objection playbook, ICP + qual
framework, competitive battlecards, security/compliance answers, proposal templates.

**Why only #2:** the "expert answer" is the *company's own* playbook → a per-customer
knowledge pack, not a shared vertical pack; and the lane is very crowded (Gong,
native meeting AI, Caretta). Strong fit, thin differentiation.

---

### 3) Consulting / professional-services & agencies — delivery meetings

**Why (grounded in YC):** YC added **"AI-native agencies/service companies"** to its
Requests for Startups, explicitly rewriting the "don't fund services" rule (analyses
cite margin shift from **20–35% → 65–80%**, and a **~$100B/yr** US consulting spend —
both *directional*). Meeting-and-action heavy (workshops, steering, status), expensive
humans ($150–500/hr), low regulation.

**Actions the avatar executes:**
1. **Decision log + action register** from steering/status calls (owner + due date).
2. **Send the client recap / "what we agreed"** email — the artifact clients ask for.
3. **Update the PM tool** (Asana/Jira/Notion): tasks, owners, milestones.
4. **Schedule** the next workshop/steering + circulate the pre-read.
5. **Flag a stalled decision** ("we'll decide next time" ≥2×) — decision-quality
   intervention (Laura's `decision_quality` template already exists).
6. **Draft the status deck / SOW-change note** from the meeting.

**Knowledge it needs:** the engagement's methodology/framework, deliverable
templates, the SOW + workstream plan, client stakeholder map, prior decisions.

**Why only #3:** pain is **diffuse** ("decisions get lost"), the "expert answer" is
the consultant's bespoke IP, and it's the weakest of the three on *one big validated
YC vertical* — YC funds the *agencies*, not tooling *for* them, so buyer-side
validation is thinner.

## The #1 recommendation

**Avatar concept: "Talia" — the AI Recruiting-Ops teammate** *(name is a placeholder;
follows the repo's human-first-name pattern — laura/cedric/marcus/duccio).*

**One-line pitch:** *Talia joins your intake and debrief calls, captures the req and
the scorecard grounded in your hiring playbook, and executes the follow-ups — JD
posted, interviews scheduled, ATS updated, submittal/recap sent — so nothing slips
between calls.*

**Target buyer (pick ONE to start):** the **founder/principal or Head of Delivery at
a boutique staffing / executive-search firm (10–100 people)**. Why this buyer over
in-house TA: their meeting time is *directly billable*, they buy fast (owner-operator,
no enterprise procurement), and staffing is global — so Laura's free multi-language is
a real edge for cross-border placement. (In-house TA at 50–500-person scaleups is a
valid second buyer, longer sales cycle.)

**The 4–6 concrete actions it ships with:**
1. Draft + post the JD from the intake call (approve-then-publish).
2. Schedule interviews / send links to candidate + panel.
3. Write the req, stage moves, and scorecard to the ATS/CRM.
4. Turn the debrief into a structured advance/reject + owner + next step; flag a
   skipped must-have once.
5. Draft the submittal (candidate → client) or prep/rejection email.
6. Chase overdue interviewer feedback; flag stalled reqs.

**The 3 (really 4) riskiest assumptions to validate with test users:**
1. **Trust/adoption:** will firms let a bot-with-a-camera into *internal* debriefs
   (candidate PII + candid panel talk)? Does its presence chill the discussion?
2. **Action-execution WTP:** is executing the follow-ups (ATS writes, JD post,
   scheduling) worth paying for — or will it be free/table-stakes once
   Ashby/Greenhouse/Bullhorn ship it natively?
3. **Ingestion cost:** do these firms have a *written* hiring playbook/rubric, or is
   it in the recruiter's head? Laura's usefulness is gated on real process docs; if
   undocumented, the onboarding cost kills the deal.
4. **Regulatory scoping:** does "internal-ops, no candidate scoring" reliably keep
   Talia OUT of the EU AI Act high-risk bucket — or do we launch US-first and revisit?

**If the owner wants to fully honor "avoid regulated AND avoid crowded":** the clean
fallback is **Consulting / professional-services delivery** (#3) — lower regulation,
less crowded — accepting weaker validation and more diffuse pain. Recruiting is the
*data-backed* pick; consulting is the *risk-minimizing* pick.
