# Methodologies, Tailoring & Governance

## Predictive, adaptive, hybrid: a decision, not a religion

**Predictive** (waterfall-style): plan deeply up front, control against
the baseline. Earns its keep when requirements are stable and the cost of
change is high; construction, regulated hardware, fixed-scope contracts.
**Adaptive** (agile family): plan shallowly and often, deliver in
increments, steer on feedback. Earns its keep when requirements are
discovered by shipping; most software. **Hybrid** is the honest reality
of most organizations: adaptive build inside predictive funding and
milestone governance. Choose per WORKSTREAM using: requirement stability,
cost of change, delivery divisibility, regulatory constraints, and team
maturity. Cargo-culting either way, sprints wrapped around a fixed spec,
or "agile" as an excuse to skip planning, buys the costs of both and the
benefits of neither.

## Tailoring

Every method is a starting kit, not a liturgy. Tailor by asking, per
practice: what risk does this ceremony/artifact retire, and do we have
that risk? A five-person team doesn't need a change control board; a
multi-vendor program does. Write the tailored method down in one page
(cadences, artifacts, decision rights) so "our process" is a fact, not an
oral tradition; then re-tailor when the project changes phase.

## Projects, programs, portfolios

A **project** delivers a defined outcome and ends. A **program**
coordinates related projects whose value only materializes together -
program management is mostly dependency and benefits management across
projects. A **portfolio** is an investment view: prioritizing all of it
against strategy and capacity. The conversations differ by layer: projects
answer "are we on plan?", programs answer "will the combined benefit
land?", portfolios answer "is this still the right money to spend?": and
killing a healthy project that no longer fits strategy is a portfolio
success, not a project failure.

## Governance that helps instead of theater

Good governance = decision rights + thresholds + cadence: who approves
scope/budget/date changes at what size, what triggers escalation
automatically, and a steering rhythm where the sponsor DECIDES things
(a steering committee that only listens is a status meeting in suits).
Stage gates work when each gate re-asks the business question ("is the
case still true?") rather than auditing document completeness. Keep the
paperwork proportional to the risk; governance weight should scale with
blast radius, not with organizational habit.

## Benefits realization: the part everyone skips

The project ships an OUTPUT; the business case promised an OUTCOME.
Someone must own the gap: name a benefits owner (usually the sponsor, not
the PM), define the measure and the review date BEFORE closure, and
actually hold the review months later. Projects whose benefits are never
measured teach the organization that business cases are admission tickets,
not commitments; which is how zombie portfolios happen. Close every
project with: acceptance confirmed, handover complete, lessons captured
where the NEXT project will find them, benefits review scheduled, team
released with thanks.
